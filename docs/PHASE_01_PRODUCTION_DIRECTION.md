# Phase 01 — Production Direction & Benchmark Strategy

Status: **ACTIVE**
Date: 2026-10-07

## 1. Purpose

Phase 01 is not a bulk asset-production phase.

Its purpose is to establish a reliable production language for Dwelling 3D assets:

- what belongs in reusable production assets;
- what exists only for Blender review;
- what should be generated by Dwelling at runtime;
- how lighting should be shared between Blender review and RealityKit;
- what visual quality is required before production is scaled.

The output of this phase is a small number of benchmark-quality assets and a repeatable workflow, not a complete apartment library.

---

## 2. Current runtime is not a fixed constraint

Dwelling's current Swift / RealityKit assets, materials, lighting and rendering implementation are still under development.

Therefore the Asset Lab must not blindly reproduce current runtime limitations.

If actual modeling and visual review reveal a better approach, the Asset Lab may propose changes to:

- asset dimensions or proportions;
- asset decomposition and module boundaries;
- pivots and canonical orientation;
- material structure;
- texture strategy;
- LOD strategy;
- lighting presets;
- runtime asset format;
- RealityKit rendering conventions;
- placement metadata.

The relationship is bidirectional:

```text
Dwelling runtime requirements
        ↓
Asset Lab experiments
        ↓
better asset / rendering knowledge
        ↓
Dwelling runtime improvements
```

The goal is one coherent production system, not two independent pipelines.

---

## 3. Three layers must remain separate

### 3.1 Review Shell

The Review Shell exists only to provide spatial context in Blender.

Examples:

- temporary walls;
- temporary floor;
- simple openings;
- temporary ceiling or cutaway geometry;
- neutral background geometry.

Rules:

- use real-world scale;
- keep geometry simple;
- do not spend hero-quality modeling time here;
- do not treat the shell as a production apartment asset;
- do not assume it will ship into Dwelling.

Dwelling should ultimately use its own wall, floor and architectural systems where appropriate.

### 3.2 Production Assets

These are reusable assets intended to survive beyond a single demo scene.

Examples:

- sofa;
- bed;
- dining table;
- chair;
- wardrobe;
- kitchen cabinet modules;
- vanity;
- lamps;
- plants;
- small props.

Production assets should be independently reusable, correctly scaled, intentionally pivoted, consistently oriented and suitable for export into the Dwelling runtime pipeline.

### 3.3 Lighting Presets

Blender lighting is a visual-development tool, not something that should be baked blindly into furniture.

A successful lighting setup should be expressed as a portable recipe that can later be reproduced or approximated in RealityKit.

Example semantic parameters:

```json
{
  "preset": "warmDaylight",
  "sunAzimuthDegrees": 225,
  "sunElevationDegrees": 42,
  "keyIntensity": 1.0,
  "environmentIntensity": 0.35,
  "fillIntensity": 0.2,
  "colorTemperatureKelvin": 5200
}
```

Exact Blender and RealityKit numeric values do not need to match. The visual intent should.

Avoid baking directional lighting into reusable furniture textures unless a future asset class explicitly requires it.

---

## 4. Apartment 001 is a benchmark scene, not a shipping asset

Apartment 001 exists to test:

- scale;
- visual consistency;
- asset composition;
- camera readability;
- lighting;
- material balance;
- runtime export;
- automated review.

It must not become a monolithic apartment model.

The current rectangular shell is only an engineering placeholder and should not be treated as the target floor plan.

Future Apartment 001 revisions may use a more credible residential layout with:

- a readable entry sequence;
- open but zoned living / dining / kitchen space;
- a private bedroom;
- a sensibly placed bathroom;
- non-trivial circulation;
- an exterior outline that does not read as a single rectangular box.

However, architectural shell detail remains secondary to reusable asset quality.

---

## 5. Visual target

Working direction:

**Warm Miniature Realism**

The target is:

- accurate real-world scale;
- believable construction;
- refined silhouettes;
- restrained stylization;
- warm residential atmosphere;
- readable materials;
- attractive presentation;
- real-time usability.

The emotional benchmark remains:

> This looks like a place I would want to live in.

The production target is not generic low-poly and not photorealism at any cost.

---

## 6. Phase 01 production strategy

Do **not** begin by building a large, highly detailed room or full apartment.

Do **not** begin by generating dozens of unrelated furniture assets either.

Use a hybrid benchmark strategy:

### Step A — Minimal Review Vignette

Create one compact review environment using placeholder architecture only.

Suggested scale:

- approximately 4–5 m wide;
- one or two simple wall planes;
- simple floor;
- one opening / daylight direction;
- enough empty space for camera movement and asset comparison.

Its only job is to provide repeatable context for scale, materials and lighting.

### Step B — Build a small set of Mother Assets

Create a small group of representative production assets at the desired final quality.

Recommended first set:

1. three-seat sofa;
2. bed with layered bedding;
3. dining table;
4. dining chair;
5. 600 mm kitchen base cabinet module;
6. sink / countertop kitchen module;
7. bathroom vanity;
8. wardrobe or storage cabinet.

These assets cover the most important modeling problems:

- hard surface;
- upholstery;
- textile;
- wood;
- painted cabinetry;
- ceramic / metal;
- modular dimensions;
- large and small silhouettes.

### Step C — Establish the lighting language

Use the mother assets in the same review vignette to establish:

- warm daylight;
- material response;
- shadow softness;
- contrast;
- practical-light behavior;
- camera exposure.

Translate the resulting visual intent into a documented Dwelling lighting preset.

### Step D — Assemble Apartment 001

Only after several mother assets pass the quality bar should they be assembled into the larger Apartment 001 benchmark scene.

At that point the apartment is used to validate:

- cross-asset consistency;
- density;
- circulation;
- room composition;
- camera angles;
- runtime performance.

This avoids spending substantial time polishing architecture before the reusable asset language is known.

---

## 7. Why furniture-first-with-context is preferred

A large room concept built first creates attractive screenshots but risks hiding weak reusable assets.

Pure furniture-first production without context creates another problem: scale, lighting and visual consistency are difficult to judge in isolation.

Therefore Phase 01 uses:

```text
small neutral review space
        +
few high-quality reusable assets
        ↓
visual + technical benchmark
        ↓
larger apartment composition
```

This keeps iteration cheap while ensuring every modeling decision can survive into production.

---

## 8. Acceptance gate before scaling production

Do not expand into a 50–70 asset library until the benchmark set demonstrates:

- believable dimensions and proportions;
- visually refined silhouettes at normal camera distance;
- no obvious "procedural block" feeling;
- consistent pivots and orientations;
- reusable materials;
- successful Blender source generation;
- successful runtime export;
- automatic validation;
- useful review renders;
- acceptable RealityKit presentation or a documented runtime improvement path.

Only then should batch production begin.

---

## 9. Immediate next milestone

**Phase 01A — Mother Asset Benchmark**

Create the minimal review vignette and build the first two high-value assets:

1. sofa;
2. bed.

Reason:

- the sofa is the best test of upholstery, bevel language and silhouette;
- the bed tests hard structure + soft textiles + layered materials;
- together they immediately reveal whether the target "Warm Miniature Realism" quality is achievable.

After those two pass visual review, continue with dining and modular cabinetry.

---

## 10. Guiding principle

Optimize for assets and knowledge that remain useful after the demo is gone.

A beautiful temporary room is useful for presentation.

A beautiful, reusable sofa with a proven material, scale, export and lighting workflow is useful for the entire Dwelling product.
